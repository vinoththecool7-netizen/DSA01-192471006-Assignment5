PURPOSE OF THE ASSIGNMENT 
Large programs must handle many kinds of objects through a common interface, and must  keep working – or fail safely – when something goes wrong. 
This assignment investigates a fundamental question: 
How should a C++ programmer design a program so that new kinds of objects can be added  without changing working code, and errors are reported and handled without losing data or  resources? 
You are expected to demonstrate the following progression: 
Abstract → Specialise → Bind at Run Time → Detect Errors → Throw → Catch → Recover  → Measure → Decide 
Simply obtaining a program that runs is not sufficient. Your design reasoning, experimental  evidence and interpretation are all part of the assessment. 
Case Study — Hospital Billing and Insurance Claims 
A multi-speciality hospital raises different kinds of bills: out-patient (consultation and tests),  in-patient (room charges per day, procedures and medicines), day-care surgery (package  price) and emergency (base charge plus time-based charges). Bills may be paid by the patient,  by an insurance company within the policy cover, or by a government health scheme with  fixed package rates. The billing system must compute every bill correctly, reject invalid  claims with clear reasons, and never lose a bill because of an error.
